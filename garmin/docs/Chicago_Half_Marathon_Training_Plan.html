Based on Garmin through Aug 16: VO2 max 49.3, half prediction 1:56:24, longest run 7.06 mi at 10:00 / 165 bpm. Easy aerobic load is short; high aerobic is over target. Most “easy” runs were actually 9:20–9:40 at 165–172 bpm.

Rules

  1. Easy means easy. Pace 10:15–10:45 and average HR ≤155. If HR is over 155, slow down even if pace slips past 10:45. The Jul 25 treadmill (4 mi @ 10:33, 149 bpm) is the template.
  2. Tempo means tempo. Continuous miles at 8:50–9:10. Warm up and cool down easy; do not average 9:20–9:32 and call it threshold. HR 165–175 belongs here, not on easy days.
  3. One hard day per week. Long run is steady, not a race. Quality is either tempo or intervals — never both, and never the day after the long.
  4. If you only have two run days, keep long + easy. Do not drop easy to fit speed. Easy aerobic is the current gap.
  5. Bike / swim / gym only on rest days, moderate, 30–45 min. They do not replace the long run or the quality session.

Hard-day sleep rule. Night before the long run and night before tempo/intervals: 8 hours in bed. If that night’s score is under 70 or HRV is clearly down, swap the quality session for an easy 4 and do not stack another hard day.

After a long run: easy or rest the next day; protect sleep that night.

Weekly structure

3 runs (Long, Quality, Easy) plus optional gym. At least one full rest or easy cross-training day between runs.

Gym (30–40 min, easy days only): squats, walking lunges, single-leg RDLs, glute bridges, planks, calf raises. Moderate. Not a second leg day.

This week — Week 5

Space these with a rest or cross day between each. Suggested order: easy → 800s → rest → long → rest.
